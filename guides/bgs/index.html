New Commander

Think in objectives, not chores.

BGS is not “run missions until the number goes up.” Decide what should happen in the system first, then choose the activity that pushes the correct faction, state, conflict, or asset.

Veteran Detail

Measure the system you actually have.

Population, competing traffic, states and diminishing returns mean there is no universal conversion from missions or credits to influence percentage. Calibrate, observe the tick, and adjust.

Starter Guide

The BGS in Six Steps

This is the mental model every Mongrel should have before touching a priority system.

01

Pick the System

Influence exists per system. Always know which system the objective applies to.

  • Check the controlling faction.
  • Check all faction influence values.
  • Check active and pending states.
02

Pick the Faction

Every positive action for one faction takes political share from the same 100% pool.

  • Know who should rise.
  • Know who may safely fall.
  • Know any retreat or expansion floors/ceilings.
03

Pick the Lever

Missions, bounties, trade, exploration data and conflict actions do different jobs.

  • Use high-INF mission rewards for influence.
  • Trade only to the intended asset owner.
  • Use combat when the active conflict requires it.
04

Set a Stop Condition

More work is not automatically better. Overshooting can trigger a conflict, Expansion or Retreat.

05

Wait for the Tick

Most results are evaluated after server processing. Third-party data can also lag until journals are uploaded.

06

Recalibrate

Compare the post-tick result with the work done, then adjust the next day’s target.

Influence

Move the Right Number for the Right Reason

Influence is a faction’s political share in one system. It is not the same thing as reputation, mission INF pips, economy, or security.

Starter rule: mission “INF +++++” is a reward weight, not five percentage points. Record mission INF separately from the faction’s displayed system influence.

MissionsTargeted positive support; high-INF rewards are efficient when influence is the goal.Read issuer, destination and target carefully. Missions can affect more than one faction/system.
Bounty vouchersPositive faction/security pressure.Voucher attribution and jurisdiction can complicate precise targeting.
Profitable tradeSupports the faction controlling the destination market and contributes economic pressure.Station ownership matters. The system controller may not own that market.
Exploration dataPositive support through a faction-controlled station.Check ownership before spending valuable data.
Crime / hostile actionsNegative pressure against a faction.Can damage security, trigger unwanted states, and create legal consequences.
Veteran notes — saturation and population

Do not assume the same workload produces the same influence swing everywhere. Population, competing commander activity and diminishing returns all matter. The correct answer is usually to calibrate the system with measured work, not to memorize a universal mission count.

Conflicts & Ownership

Influence Gets You to the Fight. Conflict Results Decide It.

When eligible factions meet in influence, they can enter War, Civil War, or Election for an asset. Once active, stop treating ordinary influence grinding as the objective.

War / Civil War

Combat conflicts. Conflict Zones and the appropriate combat actions decide daily results.

Operational mindset: win the day, verify the score, then decide whether more effort is insurance or waste.

Election

Non-combat conflict between compatible government types. Civilian/economic mission work replaces CZ fighting as the core activity.

Planning point: government type matters before you deliberately equalize two factions.

Asset Ownership

Stations, outposts, settlements and installations can belong to different factions. Conflicts transfer contested assets and can ultimately change system control.

Common mistake: forcing two factions to equal influence without first checking whether you actually want the resulting conflict, opponent, and contested asset.

Expansion & Retreat

The Dangerous Edges of the Influence Scale

The top and bottom of the influence range have strategic consequences. Treat them as thresholds with safety margins, not numbers to casually brush against.

Expansion

Community research places the trigger at roughly 75% influence. If you do not want to expand, operate below it with room for background traffic and rounding.

If you do want to expand, prepare the surrounding systems before crossing the threshold.

Targeting & Invasion

You do not simply choose the destination. Open slots, distance, previous retreats, faction count and eligibility shape the target. Full systems can turn the expansion into an invasion conflict.

Retreat

A non-native faction below roughly 2.5% can enter Retreat. Saving it requires a decisive margin; removing it requires avoiding accidental support.

Native factions cannot normally be removed from their home system this way.

Veteran notes — expansion planning

Expansion is best treated as a neighborhood-planning problem. Before triggering it, map nearby systems, faction counts, native/non-native status, current conflicts and prior faction presence. Community research on exact targeting and timing is empirical rather than a fully published Frontier formula, so use the reference entry and linked research as a planning model—not a guarantee.

Faction States

Several Things Can Be True at Once

A faction can carry multiple compatible states simultaneously. Economic, security, conflict, expansion/retreat and event conditions overlap.

Mongrel Operations

Turn Theory into Daily Orders

The guide explains mechanics. Mission Control tells you what the squad actually needs today.

A

Check Orders First

In a priority system, never assume “support the Mongrels” is automatically correct. Leadership may be intentionally holding or lowering a faction.

B

Work in Open

Mongrel rules require squad BGS work intended to influence a faction or system to be performed in Open Play.

C

Report What You Did

Useful reports separate mission INF, bounty value, trade volume, conflict results and post-tick influence percentage.
