New PvP Pilot

Survival comes before damage.

Your first goal is not to win every encounter. Learn to recognize danger, manage pips under pressure, keep control of the ship, and leave a bad fight legitimately before the rebuy screen becomes the lesson.

Mongrel Standard

Fight hard. Leave clean.

Open Play is part of the squad culture. Combat logging is not an escape method. If the fight turns against you, use the tools the game gives you: maneuver, boost, heat management, low wake, high wake, wing support, or a rebuy.

PvP Foundations

Survive, Fight, Learn

A strong PvP pilot is built in layers. Do not skip the boring parts: rebuy discipline, controls, situational awareness, pips, and repeatable practice make every advanced technique easier later.

01

Bring a Learning Ship

Use a ship you can afford to lose repeatedly. A perfect meta build is not required to learn spacing, pip rhythm, boost timing, aim, and disengagement.

  • Keep multiple rebuys available.
  • Know your boost speed and jump controls before the fight.
  • Use a loadout simple enough that you can focus on flying.
  • Change one major variable at a time while learning.
02

Control Before Damage

The pilot who decides distance, closure, and angle usually gets more useful weapon time than the pilot with the larger paper-DPS number.

  • Keep the nose working without blindly chasing the target.
  • Manage closure before it turns into another head-on pass.
  • Use vertical and lateral thrust, not pitch alone.
  • Boost with a reason: turn, close, extend, or escape.
03

Debrief Every Fight

A loss with a clear lesson is more valuable than ten chaotic fights you cannot explain.

  • What range favored you?
  • Where did your pips fall apart?
  • Did you lose aim, position, shields, modules, or resources first?
  • Pick one correction and test it in the next round.
Beginner rule: if you cannot explain why you died, do not immediately rebuild the ship. Recreate the fight with a squadmate and identify the first repeatable mistake.

Open-Play Survival

Recognize Trouble Before It Becomes a Fight

PvP starts before hardpoints deploy. Read the contacts around you, know your escape route, and decide early whether you are fighting, running, or buying time for help.

Before You Undock

Rebuy: never treat insurance as optional. A combat ship you are afraid to lose is a poor training platform.

Route: know which nearby system you can high wake to if you need a clean exit.

Bindings: have boost, pips, target-next-hostile, subsystem targeting, heatsinks, chaff, shield cells, and FSD controls where you can reach them under pressure.

Interdiction

If you want the fight, commit deliberately. If you do not, decide quickly whether to contest or submit.

A deliberate submission gives a much faster recovery than losing the interdiction, which can make it easier to boost, charge the FSD, and escape before sustained damage begins.

Do not wait until the ship is already failing to decide that the encounter was unwinnable.

After the Drop

Put defensive pips in immediately, boost with purpose, identify the attacker, and start the escape or counterattack you already chose.

Watch for wingmates entering the instance. A manageable 1v1 can become a bad 1v3 very quickly.

Situational awareness is part of defense; the radar is not decoration.

Veteran notes — choosing the fight

Experienced Open pilots are not obligated to accept every engagement. Ship matchup, wing size, available ammunition, shield-cell state, nearby high-wake options, crime response, and the objective you were originally trying to complete all matter.

A clean escape is not cowardice. Staying in a hopeless fight until the ship explodes is only useful when you are deliberately practicing that situation.

Power Distributor

Pips Are a Combat Control, Not a Setup Choice

Static 2-2-2 pips waste one of the strongest tools on the ship. Move power as the problem changes: survive pressure, gain position, feed weapons, then move it again.

Starter rhythm: under heavy incoming shield damage, bias SYS. When you need movement, bias ENG. When you have a firing window, feed WEP enough to sustain it. Then reassess.

SYS — Stay Alive

SYS pips substantially improve shield resilience while shields are up and refill the SYS capacitor used by defensive systems.

When an opponent has a clean firing window on you, defensive pips are often worth more than squeezing out a little extra damage.

ENG — Own the Geometry

ENG feeds boost and improves the ship's movement authority. Use it when you need to turn, close, extend, break a joust, or reposition around the opponent.

Running the ENG capacitor empty at the wrong moment can remove your best option for fixing a bad position.

WEP — Sustain the Window

WEP keeps the weapon capacitor healthy. A drained capacitor can collapse sustained damage and make energy-heavy weapons run hot or become difficult to maintain.

Do not park four pips in WEP while the opponent is freely damaging you. Damage only matters if you live long enough to apply it.

Practice drill — pip rhythm

Fight a squadmate for several rounds where the goal is not the kill. Call out your own pip changes: “SYS under pressure,” “ENG to reposition,” “WEP for the window.” The verbal step is temporary, but it forces you to notice why you are moving power instead of doing it by habit.

Movement & Positioning

Point the Ship Where the Fight Is Going

Human opponents punish predictable turns. Good movement combines pitch, yaw, roll, vertical/lateral thrust, throttle, boost timing, and selective Flight Assist Off.

Stop Flying Flat

Use vertical and lateral thrusters while turning. Sliding the ship changes the line of approach and can keep your weapons on target without flying directly into the opponent's nose.

Roll to put the strongest pitch axis where you need it rather than asking yaw to solve everything.

Boost With a Job

Boost can increase closure, create separation, sharpen a turn, recover a bad angle, or begin a disengagement. Random boost spam often creates overshoots and drains ENG when you need it most.

Before pressing boost, know whether you are trying to close, turn, extend, or leave.

Flight Assist Off

FA Off lets the ship's nose move more independently from its current velocity vector. That is useful for reversals, tracking through a pass, lateral drift, and maintaining weapon pressure while the ship continues moving another direction.

Start with short toggled bursts. Full-time FA Off is not a prerequisite for effective PvP.

Veteran notes — movement is matchup dependent

There is no universal “best turn.” A nimble fixed-weapon ship, a heavy shield tank, a fragment build, and a rail/plasma platform all want different geometry. The goal is not maximum rotation at all times; it is to create the next favorable firing window while denying the opponent theirs.

Range Control

Fight in Your Envelope, Not Theirs

Range control is the difference between merely pointing at someone and actually dictating the engagement.

A

Know Your Preferred Range

Your weapons, projectile speed, falloff, convergence, ship agility, and opponent's loadout all influence the distance you want.

  • Fragment and short-range packages want reliable close access.
  • Rails reward stable precision windows.
  • Plasma rewards prediction and controlled lead.
  • Mixed packages may have more than one useful envelope.
B

Manage Closure

If every engagement becomes a full-speed head-on pass, neither pilot is controlling range very well.

  • Reduce closure before the merge.
  • Use lateral movement instead of chasing directly.
  • Extend only far enough to reset the geometry.
  • Do not surrender a good position by boosting past the target.
C

Read Their Intent

Human pilots advertise what they want through throttle, boost timing, nose position, and repeated approach patterns.

  • If they keep extending, ask what range they are trying to create.
  • If they force face-tanks, decide whether the damage trade favors you.
  • If they orbit outside your effective range, stop feeding the pattern.

Weapon Application

Paper DPS Does Not Win Missed Shots

PvP rewards applied damage: choosing the right window, keeping the target inside the weapon's useful envelope, and using engineering effects deliberately.

Fixed Weapons

Fixed weapons reward prediction and ship control. Practice following the target's movement instead of reacting only to where the reticle is now.

Rails favor short precision windows. Plasma requires leading a moving target and learning projectile travel time. Both punish panic firing.

Gimbals & Chaff

Gimbals reduce aiming workload but can be disrupted by chaff. Against chaff, consider delaying the shot, changing the angle, or using weapons that do not rely on assisted tracking.

Chaff does not make the target invulnerable; it attacks the tracking solution.

Utility Effects

Some PvP weapons are valuable because of what they do to the opponent rather than their raw damage. Feedback Cascade can pressure shield-cell use; Drag can interfere with movement; Dispersal and Target Lock Breaker can disrupt weapon or target control.

Build the package around a plan instead of stacking unrelated experimentals.

Engineering crossover: for weapon blueprint and experimental guidance, use the Engineering Guide. This manual focuses on applying the package against another Commander.
Veteran notes — subsystem pressure

Once shields fail, subsystem targeting can end a fight faster than chewing through every point of hull. Drives, power plant, weapons, and other internals can become tactical targets depending on the ship and your weapon package.

Do not tunnel-vision on the module panel. If precision targeting destroys your position or causes you to eat free damage, return to the larger fight first.

Defensive Flying

Make Their Damage Difficult to Apply

Defense is more than shield strength. Position, pips, module protection, heat, utility timing, and knowing when the exchange is going bad all matter.

Shields & Cells

Time Shield Cell Banks around the fight rather than treating them like a panic button. Cells generate heavy heat and can be disrupted by Feedback Cascade rails, so watch the opponent's firing window and your own thermal state.

Heatsinks are often paired with cell use to manage the thermal spike.

Hull & Modules

When shields fall, Hull Reinforcement keeps the ship alive while Module Reinforcement helps protect the internals that let you continue fighting or escape.

If the drives, power plant, canopy, or FSD are repeatedly ending your fights, treat that as a build or positioning problem worth diagnosing.

Heat & Silent Running

Silent Running closes the radiators and suppresses your normal thermal signature, but the ship continues generating heat internally. It is a tactical state, not free invisibility.

Use it with a purpose: breaking or complicating target tracking, buying time during shield-down movement, or supporting a planned disengagement. Watch heat closely.

Veteran notes — defensive tools should buy position

Chaff, sinks, cells, silent running, reversals, and boosts are strongest when they create a useful next state. Ask what the defensive action is buying: time, distance, broken tracking, shield recovery, a firing angle, or the start of an escape.

Legitimate Disengagement

Know How to Leave Before You Need To

Disengagement is a PvP skill. Practice it in controlled fights so the sequence is automatic when the hull is actually disappearing.

Create the Window

Bias ENG and SYS as needed, boost to create separation or a difficult firing angle, and use defensive utilities to reduce incoming pressure.

Do not wait for the FSD to solve a bad position by itself. Your flying creates the charge window.

Low Wake vs High Wake

Low waking returns to supercruise in the current system. A much heavier nearby ship can make that option difficult through mass lock.

A hyperspace jump to another system avoids that same mass-lock problem and is often the more reliable escape when a larger attacker is sitting on you.

Combat Logging

Closing the game or disconnecting to avoid a legitimate combat outcome is not part of the Mongrel escape playbook.

If the ship cannot escape through game mechanics, accept the rebuy, learn from the sequence, and fix the problem before the next encounter.

Practice drill: have a squadmate start from an attacking position. Your only objective is to survive long enough to complete a legitimate high wake. Repeat until the sequence stops feeling rushed.

PvP Builds

The Ship Should Support the Lesson

You do not need one mandatory hull to learn PvP. You do need a coherent build whose weapons, defenses, distributor, speed, and intended engagement range agree with one another.

BUILD

Learning Platform

Prioritize affordability, predictable handling, enough durability to survive mistakes, and a weapon package simple enough to understand.

  • A-rated or engineered core modules where practical.
  • Reliable boost access.
  • Balanced defenses with module protection.
  • Weapons that teach the skill you actually want to practice.
TEST

Benchmark the Problem

Do not rebuild after every loss. Use the same opponent or drill long enough to identify what is actually failing.

  • Can't stay on target? Test movement and range first.
  • Capacitors collapse? Review pips and distributor demand.
  • Modules die early? Review protection and shield-down flying.
  • Can't finish kills? Review application before adding DPS.
REFINE

Specialize Later

Once the fundamentals are stable, specialize for dueling, wing pressure, fixed weapons, reverski counters, shield-cell pressure, or a particular hull matchup.

  • Know what the specialization gives up.
  • Keep the escape plan intact.
  • Test changes against human opponents, not only paper stats.

Organized Duels

Controlled Fights Build Skills Faster

A squadmate who will reset the same situation ten times is a better teacher than ten random interdictions you never get to examine.

Debrief format

Keep the debrief short: What worked? What failed first? What one change do we test next? Avoid burying a developing pilot under twenty corrections after one fight.

Wing PvP

Four Pilots Acting Together Beat Four Separate Duels

Wing fighting changes target priority, survivability, movement, and communication. The goal is not to prove who can solo the opponent first.

Focus Fire

One clear target call is worth more than four pilots distributing damage across the entire enemy wing.

Call the Commander name, confirm the switch, and pressure the same target until the tactical reason changes.

Mutual Support

Watch which teammate is being focused. A wingmate under heavy pressure may need peel, counter-pressure, a target switch, or room to disengage.

Do not chase one low target so far away that the rest of your wing loses support.

Comms

Keep calls short and useful: target, shields, cell use, disengaging, rejoining, or immediate danger.

Long explanations belong in the debrief. During the fight, information must arrive before it expires.

Wing rule: if everyone is calling targets, nobody is calling targets. Establish who owns primary calls before the engagement begins.

Advanced PvP

Diagnose the Matchup, Then Change the Fight

Advanced PvP is less about collecting tricks and more about recognizing why the current engagement favors one pilot.

READ

Identify the Opponent's Plan

Watch the repeated pattern. Are they forcing face-tanks, orbiting outside your range, reverskiing, draining your cells, breaking locks, or trying to isolate you from the wing?

ADAPT

Change One Variable

Change closure, vertical offset, boost timing, target priority, pip rhythm, or weapon timing. If the same sequence keeps failing, stop repeating it faster.

LEAD

Teach the Cause, Not the Trick

A PvP lead should be able to explain why a maneuver, build choice, or target call works, then help another pilot recognize the same situation without being prompted.

Veteran notes — leadership standard

Before an organized fight, define the objective, target-call ownership, regroup point, escape condition, and whether the session is training, competition, or support for a squad objective. During the debrief, separate pilot error, build limitation, matchup disadvantage, and command error instead of blaming the nearest obvious mistake.
